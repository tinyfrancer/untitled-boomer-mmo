# The spirit

The helper that floats beside the player and is a character in the story (decision 87), which A9's
tips spoke for first and D4 drew in the world. This is who it is. The player learns it in the
order below, and not faster.

## What it is

**Wick** is a kindled soul: a light about the size of a fist, blue-white, floating at the player's
shoulder. The blue is the one A9's tip card is edged in. It gives light enough to read by and no
heat. It cannot touch anything, cannot go far from the player, and cannot be hurt by anything the
player can see.

It calls itself Wick because when it came down the hill it did not remember its name, and the one
thing it knew about itself was that it was burning.

## Who it was

**Lorn**, lampwright to Merrath the Last. A lamp-warden's child, taught young to tend the kings'
lanterns in their barrows, the best at it there had ever been, and made the king's lampwright young
for it. Lorn believed what the king said: that the Great Kindling would keep everyone, the king and
the wardens and the dead kings, the whole kingdom in one light, and nobody would ever have to die
again. Lorn made the lantern the dwarves would not, and lit it.

The bible does not say whether Lorn was a man or a woman. Wick does not remember, and nobody left
alive knew Lorn.

After the Drowning the survivors found Lorn alive, and kindled Lorn, unwilling, into a small lantern
sealed in a cell in the Greyhills, far from the sea (`history.md`). Six hundred years in the dark, in
a lantern nobody tended, wore away everything but the light, which is why Wick remembers nothing.

## Why it is with the player

A soul out of its lantern is drawn to Marhal, and a soul close to a living one is held against the
pull by its warmth. When the New Cut's blast cracked the lantern, Wick felt the pull at once, fled
downhill from it, and fastened on the nearest warm thing: the player, asleep at the Wet Boot on their
first night in Lampton.

What Wick says about it at first: "You were the nearest warm thing. Don't take it personally."

What it says when it is ready to say the truth (Part G): "I didn't pick you because you were warm. I
picked you because if I hadn't, I'd have gone to him."

## What it wants

- **At first**, to know what it is. It is curious about everything, delighted to be out, and a
  little vain.
- **As it remembers**, to know what it did, and it is more and more afraid of the answer.
- **At the end**, to undo it.

What it does not want is to go near the sea. It feels the pull there, and does not know what the
pull is until late.

**Where a memory is a thing, the thing says it.** Five of the nine are found rather than arrived
at: the stone, the cell, the coin, the ring and the door are secrets, and Wick's line on finding
each is the memory. Its line on arriving in those zones only leads up to it, never says it first,
so a player who never finds the thing has the zone's unease and not its answer.

**Its waking comes first, wherever the player is**, and is the one line it says without being
asked: "You were the nearest warm thing, so here I am. Don't take it personally. I'm Wick, and when
I glow, tap me: I'll have something to say." Everything after waits for a tap, the light glowing until it gets one.

**The Willow Warden is not in the game yet**, so her beat is Wick's alone: a word said at it from
the willows that it cannot see the speaker of. **Maren is, since D1b**, and the fen's beat is now
the lead-up to her: the knowledge coming on Wick on arriving like a burn, that people like the
lantern-keepers put it in the hill, and then Maren, asked about the light, saying it to its face
("We put you in the hill"). Wick's answer, "Why would you do that?", is the player's to ask her,
and she does not say.

**When it has nothing to remember, it talks about where it is**: two lines a zone, taken in turn,
which remember nothing and never run ahead of the table.

## How it speaks

- **Bright, curious and a little vain, early on.** "I'm a very good light. You may say so."
- **Kind.** It likes the player, and people generally, and it is on the player's side even when it
  is frightened.
- **Short.** One or two sentences. It never explains the history wholesale, and it is sometimes
  wrong about what it is remembering.
- **Quieter as it remembers.** Its jokes thin as the band rises, which is the tone's rule for
  everything (`tone.md`).
- **It teaches plainly.** A tip names the control it means, "Tap it and Eat", because being clear
  matters more than staying in the world (pillar 1). It never jokes about being in a game.

A9's tips are already near enough its voice ("Hold a finger on anything... I'll tell you what I
know."). D4 gave them its name and its light: each waits in Wick until it is tapped.

## While the player rests

Wick does not sleep. Six hundred years in the dark left it with no wish to shut out anything ever
again, and it is not sure it could. So when the player stops, idling at the edge of a rat run or
putting the game down for the night, Wick keeps watch: it sits a little brighter at their shoulder,
counts what goes by, and keeps the worst of the dark off them, and the player comes back to the
road rested for it. This is rested XP (phase E1, `docs/v2_plan.md`): a bonus the time idle or away
banks, spent on what the player then earns by hand. It is the one thing Wick can do for the warm body
that holds it against the pull, and it is a little proud of it. When it says anything about it, it
is short and pleased with itself: "You slept. I didn't. Off you go."

## The ending, and why

As intended (`history.md`): Wick goes into the great light and lets go, and every soul of Veymar
goes on with it, Lorn's too. The bible wants it because the whole story is about keeping and letting
go. Veymar kept its dead and drowned for it; the fenfolk keep theirs and pay for it with their own;
the Company keeps going and breaks what it does not see; and the one soul that caused all of it is
the one that can let go of all of it. The player climbs from nobody to the one who carried Wick down
to Marhal, and walks back up alone, having earned it. Whether the player can argue with Wick, and
what the last of its lines are, is Part G's.
