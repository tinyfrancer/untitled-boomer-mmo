# Naming

How each people names things, so a new name sounds as if it belongs and a reader can tell who named
it. Then every name already taken.

## Settlers

- **Places** are plain English for what the settlers saw or did there: Greyford, Blackwater Fen, the
  Deep Cut, the New Cut, Candle Strand, Lampton. When they found an old name they heard it as
  English, so Veymar became the Veymarch.
- **People** have short, old-fashioned given names and a family name from a trade, a place or a
  habit: Tilda Pell, Jory Stroud, Marta Hale, Silas Quill, Hollis Crane, Cobb Harrow. The given name
  among friends, the family name in a ledger.
- **Inns** are a thing and a joke: the Wet Boot.
- **Oaths** are rare: "Crown's teeth", "hearth and harrow".

## The old tongue

Veymari, the language of Veymar, spoken now only by the fenfolk as words mixed into common, and
written on stones nobody else can read. Its names are built from a handful of roots, stressed on the
first syllable, and it has no k, g or z.

| Root | Meaning                                  | In              |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| vey  | light                                    | Veymar          |
| mar  | sea                                      | Veymar, Marhal  |
| hal  | hill, and a barrow, which is a hill made | Marhal, Orlhal  |
| orl  | gold                                     | Orlath, Orlhal  |
| mer  | all                                      | Merrath         |
| ath  | holder, lord: a king's ending            | Orlath, Merrath |
| ess  | sleep, rest                              | Essmar          |
| lor  | keep, tend                               | Lorn, lortir    |
| tir  | people                                   | lortir          |

- **Part G's** old-tongue names are built from the roots above and add none: Lorhal "keeping-hill",
  Essath "sleep-holder", Halath "hill-holder", Veyath "light-holder", and Vessa, a warden's name worn
  down from vey and ess.
- **Kings** end in -ath: Orlath is "gold-holder", Merrath "all-holder". Settlers who half know the
  tongue think Merrath means "sea-king", and are wrong in a way that matters.
- **Places** join two roots: Marhal "sea-hill", Orlhal "gold-hill", and Essmar "the sleeping sea",
  which is what the fenfolk call the fen.
- **People** have a root worn down to a name: Lorn ("tended"), Maren, Tirrow, Essa, Orla, Vey.
  **A fenfolk has one name** and is known by the light they keep: Maren, keeper of the third light.
- A new word adds its root to the table in the same change.

## Elves

- Long and soft: three syllables or more, no hard stops (no b, d, g, k, p or t), and the vowels
  doubled (ae, ie, ei): Ilvaeris.
- An elf does not give its name. It gives a human something to call it, from what the human saw it
  as: the Willow Warden. Its own name is given once, to somebody it trusts.
- What the elves call the Stillwood is not known. "The Stillwood" is the settlers' name for it, since
  no bird sings there.

## Dwarves

- Short and hard: one or two syllables, k, g, d and r, the consonants doubled: Karra, Brunn, Dagg,
  Holt.
- A given name, and a byname from the dwarf's craft or where they work: Karra Deepvein.
- Karn Tholl is "stone hall". A dwarf names the things they make, so a dwarven blade has a name of
  its own.

## Goblins

- Short, guttural, and chosen by the goblin: Gorb, Snikk, Ugwort, Grizzle, Nobbin. A goblin who does
  something worth boasting about changes it.
- A knot is named for its biggest (Gorb's knot), and a tangle for its Big One (Big Gorb).

## Creatures, items, titles and zones

- **Creatures** are plain English for what they are and where: Bog Lurker, Cave Crawler, Barrow
  Wight, Fen Raider, Goblin Scavenger. A named foe is a name and an epithet: Hollis the Cutthroat,
  Orlath the Barrow King.
- **Items** are a material or tier and the thing (Iron Helmet, Steel Arrows), or where or whom it
  came from and the thing (Fenweave Robe, Barrow Blade, Goblin Maul, Grave Lantern). A unique item may
  carry its maker's or its owner's name.
- **Titles** are a rank word after what the rank is in (Rat Culler, Goblin Slayer). A faction's
  titles are in its own words (D3).
- **Zones** take "The" where a settler would say it (The Deep Cut, The Sunken Barrow) and not where
  the name is a proper name (Lampton, Greyford Outpost).

## No borrowed names

No name is borrowed from another world, Tolkien's, Azeroth, Gielinor or anybody's. A new name is
checked against the list below and against the obvious sources before it goes in, and added to the
list in the same change.
