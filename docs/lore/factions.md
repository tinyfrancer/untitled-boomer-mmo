# Factions

Who wants what in the Veymarch, and who stands against whom. Phase D3 turns these into standing the
player earns, moved by quests, kills, contracts and dialog (decision 87). What the bible settles is
who exists and what they want; what standing opens, and whether raising one lowers another, is
D3's.

There are five factions a player can earn standing with, and three kinds of enemy that only ever
fight.

## The Veymarch Company

**Seat:** Lampton, the Quartermaster's Post. **Also:** the New Cut, the salt pans, Greyford's yard.
**Face:** Jory Stroud, the quartermaster. **Colour:** red.

The chartered company settling the west for the Crown of Aldmark. It wants the Veymarch safe and
paying: timber, ore, salt, and roads a cart can use. It is not the villain. It pays fairly, keeps
order and builds well, and it does not know what it is breaking; told, most of its people do not
believe it, and a few believe it and do not care. The contract board is the Company's: rats, raiders,
timber and ore.

Standing could open better contracts, Company stock and a Company title. The Company already lets
its empty houses to whoever is useful: the Surveyor's House in Lampton is the player's once the Red
Rags are broken and its roof mended (`places.md`; _in game_ since F1).

## The Keepers

**Seat:** Lorhal, a holm hidden in the deep fen west of Blackwater Fen (Part G). **Faces:** Maren, keeper of the third light, an
elder; Tirrow, who leads the raiders.

The fenfolk as a people. They want the lanterns kept, the barrows shut, and the Company off the fen,
its pans filled in and its drains stopped. They will deal with anybody who helps them keep their
dead, and they are the only people left who know the kindling.

Standing could open fenweave and fen bows, the way to the holm, the barrows' keys and lanterns, and
at the top, what they did to Lorn.

## Greyford

**Seat:** Greyford Outpost. **Faces:** Oona Rook, the outfitter; Silas Quill, the fettler.

The free traders at the Company's forward post. The yard is the Company's in name and theirs in fact,
and they pay the Company to keep the road open. They barter rather than take coin, deal with anybody
who brings something worth having (the fenfolk quietly, goblins very quietly), and ask no questions.
The fettler fences grave goods: the crowns and blades that come out of barrows are reworked at his
bench, and the cracked lantern from the New Cut is in his back room.

Standing could open better trades, rarer reforges, and the back room.

## The Quiet Court

**Seat:** the Quiet Court, at the heart of the Stillwood west past Greyford (Part G). **Face in levels 1-8:** the Willow Warden.

The elves. They want every kindled soul to go on, the kindling ended for good, and nobody in the
Stillwood. They disapprove of the fenfolk, who kindle their own dead however good the reason, and of
Wick. How they judge the player depends on Wick.

## Karn Tholl

**Seat:** Karn Tholl's gate hall, behind the sealed door in the Deep Cut (Part G). **Face in levels 1-8:** Karra
Deepvein, a scout.

The dwarves. They want their drowned halls back, the goblins out of their workings, their debt paid,
and their work respected, which means returned.

## Enemies only

- **The Red Rags**, Hollis Crane's gang on the east road. Hollis guarded a Company pay-cart until
  eight years ago, when he drove it off the road instead, found a Veymari waystation with a vault
  under it, and has been robbing carts from it since, and lately graves. His men wear strips torn
  from Company red, as a joke at the Company's expense. They have no grievance but greed.
- **The goblin knots** fight anything in their holes. A goblin who trades with the player is
  possible later; a goblin faction is not.
- **The drowned dead**, wights and barrow kings, whom nobody deals with.

## How they stand towards each other

- **The Company and the Keepers are opposed**: over the fen, the pans and the lanterns. It is the
  realm's one real opposition, and the reason the raiders are more than enemies.
- **The Keepers and the Court distrust each other** over the kindling. Each thinks the other is
  wrong about the dead.
- **Karn Tholl and the goblins are enemies** over the workings, and Karn Tholl will be the fettler's
  when it finds out what he does for a living.
- **Greyford trades with everyone**, which is why nobody trusts it and everybody uses it.
- **The Company does not believe in the Court or Karn Tholl**, until it has to.

## Standing and ranks

_In game since D3 (decision 133)._ The Company, the Keepers and Greyford hold standing before level
9; the Court and Karn Tholl are met once each, and their standing opens in Part G.

- **The Company and the Keepers are opposed on deeds, not a seesaw.** A raider killed pays the
  Company and costs the Keepers; taking a side over the salt pans with Jory Stroud moves them apart;
  every other deed moves one alone.
- **The upper chain's ten raiders cost the Keepers**, and putting Orlath down earns it back: the
  Keepers call it a mercy, his lantern being out and nothing else able to lay him. A barrow wight laid
  is a smaller mercy of the same kind.
- **Ranks, in each faction's own words**, each above a stranger's a title:
  - The Company counts in its ledger: Stranger, **Company Hand**, **Company Contractor**, **Company
    Factor**. A factor is what the Company calls the agent who keeps its books in a place, and it
    does not hand the word out.
  - The Keepers: **Drainer**, their word for anybody who digs the fen's drains and pans and so for
    the Company's people; Outsider, which is everybody else; **Guest of the Fen**; **Lightfriend**;
    and **Fenkin**, the most they say of anybody not born on a holm.
  - Greyford: Stranger, **Greyford Regular**, **Greyford Trader**, **Friend of the Yard**, which the
    yard has never once said to the Company.
- **What a rank opens before 9**: the Post salts down eel for its own carters and sells it to a
  Company Contractor; Oona Rook keeps the coal trade for a Greyford Regular; Silas Quill admits to the
  lantern in his back room to a Greyford Trader; and Jory Stroud and Oona speak differently to
  somebody they count.
