# Peoples

Who lives in the Veymarch, how many of them, what they want and how they talk. Humans are nearly
everyone. The rest are rare, and meeting one should be an event (pillar 4).

## Settlers

The Company's people, from Aldmark: younger children with no land coming to them, debtors, old
soldiers, widows, chancers, anybody who could put a mark on a Company paper and sit on a cart for a
week. They came for land, for wages, or to be somewhere nobody knew them. There are a few thousand
of them in the Veymarch, most in and around Lampton.

They are practical, loud and hard-working, and brave the way people are who have not met anything
worse than a rat yet. They are superstitious in private and scornful of it in public. They think
the fenfolk are thieves, the elves a story, the dwarves long dead and the goblins vermin with
opinions, and they are wrong about three of the four.

They talk plainly and drily, with the odd oath ("Crown's teeth"), and a settler's joke is usually
at somebody's expense, their own included.

## Fenfolk

The last people of Veymar, and the reason anybody still lives in the fen: the lamp-wardens'
families who survived the Drowning and stayed. There are a few hundred of them on the holms of high
ground in Blackwater Fen, in stilt-houses reached by punt, living on eels, reed and sheep, and
weaving **fenweave**, the finest cloth in the Veymarch, from bog-cotton and nettle. They call
themselves the **lortir**, the keepers. The settlers call them fenfolk when they are being polite,
and raiders when they are not.

They keep the barrows' lanterns lit and give their dead to them (`history.md`), and everything else
about them follows from that. They are grave, patient and proud, say little and mean all of it, and
keep a bleak humour among themselves. They carry the barrows' keys. They have not forgotten what Lorn
did, and they have not forgiven it.

Their young are angry. The salt pans cut them off from their dead and the Company will not listen,
so the young strike the pans, the strand's fishers and anybody on the fen they do not know, which
includes the player. Those are the **fen raiders**. Their elders do not approve, and do not stop
them.

They speak common with words of the old tongue in it, and a fenfolk is known by the light they keep.

## Elves

The elves are **the Quiet Court**, and there are very few of them, fewer than before Veymar. They
live longer than anybody has counted; some of them walked Marhal's streets. Their magic is memory
and growing things, and the rite of the parting candle, which they taught Veymar and have regretted
since. To an elf a soul must go on, the kindling is the worst thing humans have ever done, and a
kindled soul is a horror and a grief at once. They withdrew into the Stillwood before the Drowning
and shut it after.

What brings them out now is the lanterns going out, because every soul that goes to Marhal is a soul
that does not go on. The Court wants every one of them to go on. It is not sure the settlers, the
fenfolk or Wick deserve its help getting there.

An elf looks like a person until you notice they have not blinked. They speak rarely, in the present
tense and without contractions, and never joke, and they find humans funny, which is worse. They do
not give their names: they give a human something to call them.

**In levels 1-8 there is one elf**, the Willow Warden at the millpond (`places.md`), and meeting her
is one of the rarest things in the band.

## Dwarves

The dwarves of **Karn Tholl**, under the Greyhills and the mountains north of them. They made
Veymar's lanterns, crowns and blades, and their mark is on every grave good that comes out of a
barrow. They refused to make the Great Kindling's lantern, lost their deepest halls to the sea
anyway, and shut their doors six hundred years ago. The settlers think they are extinct.

They are blunt, proud of their own work and contemptuous of anybody else's, and they keep accounts of
everything, debts above all: Veymar owes them for the halls, and by their reckoning whoever stands in
Veymar now has inherited the debt. They are funny without meaning to be and do not like being laughed
at. A dwarf names the things they make.

What brings them out is the digging: goblins in their outer workings for a century, and now the
Company breaking into the Deep Cut above them. They want their halls back, their debt paid and their
work respected, which means returned, since every crown the fettler reworks was theirs first.

**In levels 1-8 there is one dwarf**, a scout come up through a door in the Deep Cut to see who is
digging (`places.md`).

## Goblins

The goblins came down into the empty Greyhills after the dwarves shut their doors, and dig where the
dwarves stopped. They live in **knots** of three, each run by its biggest, and knots gather into a
**tangle** under a **Big One** when there is something worth fighting for. They see in the dark,
which is why their eyes are lit; they found the coal before anybody; and they scavenge Veymar's ruins
and the Company's carts for anything shiny, which is how most Veymari gold that turns up has been
through a goblin's hands first.

They are not wicked. They are hungry, territorial, proud and easily offended, very brave in threes
and not at all in ones. They are the realm's comedy, and they still bite. They will not go near a
barrow: goblins know what is in barrows.

A goblin speaks common well enough to insult you in it, and talks about itself by name when there is
something to boast about.

## The dead

- **Wights** are the bodies the barrow lanterns kept, woken when their lanterns go out and their
  souls go to Marhal: empty, cold, and hungry for warmth, which is why they go for the living. They
  remember nothing but where they were laid and what they were buried holding.
- **Barrow kings** woke the same way with a little of themselves left in their crowns and blades:
  enough to speak a few words of the old tongue, to command wights, and to remember a face.
- They were people, and they are the realm's real stakes. The fenfolk mourn them, the settlers fear
  them and the goblins avoid them, and nobody jokes about them but goblins and fools.

## Stranger things

Each is rare, met in one place, and never named before it is seen. The rumours in `places.md` lead
to them.

- **Strays** are loose souls on their way to Marhal: small lights drifting low over the fen at
  night, always south. They are Wick's kin, though it will not say so. (Blackwater Fen)
- **The white stag** walks the Old Mill Road at dusk: the Willow Warden's messenger, or the Willow
  Warden. (Old Mill Road)
- **The light under the sea**, far out past the Candles on a dark night, is Merrath's great light
  seen from above. (Candle Strand, from the shore)
- **Pocket**, the crow on the longhouse roof at Greyford, talks, is rude, and knows things it should
  not. Nobody knows why it talks. It says it is not telling. (Greyford)
- **Cave crawlers** are sea crabs gone pale in the dark, and there are no crabs in the Greyhills: they
  came up through the dwarves' drowned halls, which means those halls still reach the sea. (The Deep
  Cut)
- **Dragons** are real, and there are very few. The dwarves know of one, asleep in the north, and do
  not go near it. (Part G)

The rest of what lives in the Veymarch is only animals, however it looks: rats, crabs, eels, and the
fen's **bog lurkers**, newts the size of dogs whose hides the wet cannot get through.

## Where each class comes from

The three classes are three ways a nobody earns a living on a frontier. Part G's specialisations
(decision 84) can grow out of these; they are hooks, not choices.

- **Warriors** are Aldmark's soldiers left over from its wars, the Company's militia and the
  sellswords it hires. Marta Hale trains them in Lampton. A path that holds a line and a path that
  breaks one are both at home here.
- **Wizards** are trained in Aldmark's hedge-colleges in fire, the one magic humans kept after
  Veymar, and are taught that fire is not the kindling: a burning with nobody in it. Settlers are a
  little wary of wizards, and the fenfolk more, since fire and lanterns do not mix. A wizard's Mana
  Shield and Mend already lean towards light that wards, and a path that goes further that way edges
  towards the kindling: a temptation the story can use.
- **Rangers** are frontier hunters, poachers and scouts, and the best bows in the Veymarch are
  fen-made. A path that hunts the fen's way (the marsh, traps, patience) and a path that shoots
  Aldmark's (range, the volley) are both at home here.
