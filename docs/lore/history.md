# History

The realm, what it was, what broke it, and what is happening in it now. `places.md` has where each
of these things happened, and `spirit.md` the part of it that is Wick's.

## The realm's name

**The Veymarch** (VAY-march) is the country west of Aldmark: the Greyhills to the north, the sea to
the south, the Stillwood to the west, and the east road back to Aldmark's last towns, a week away
by cart. It is what the settlers made of the name they found here, **Veymar** (VAY-mar), hearing a
march in it, a frontier, which is what the land is to them. It was a kingdom. The fenfolk still call
the land Veymar, and mean the half of it under the water as well.

## Veymar

Veymar was a human kingdom in the low country between the Greyhills and the sea, most of it lower
than the sea at high tide, and it stood for nine hundred years because of one thing: **the
kindling**.

The elves had a rite older than the kingdom. A candle lit at a deathbed holds a little of the one
who has died, for a night: long enough to say goodbye, and then it is let go out. Veymar learned the
rite from them, and learned to stop the candle going out. A soul given to a flame could be bound
into it, and a kindled flame **holds**: whatever it is set to keep, it keeps for as long as it burns
and is tended.

- **The sea-lights** stood along the coast, a tower every mile or so with a kindled lamp at the top,
  and together they held the sea back from the low country. Each had a warden who tended it living
  and gave themselves to it dying. It was the highest honour in Veymar, and Veymar means **light on
  the sea**.
- **The barrow kings** were kindled too. A king was laid in a barrow with his crown and his blade
  and his lantern beside him, and while the lantern burned he slept in his body and was awake in the
  flame, where his heirs could go down and ask him things. Veymar was ruled by its living and
  advised by its dead. A king's name ends in **-ath**, "holder".
- **The waymarkers** on the roads each held a small light for travellers.
- **The lamp-wardens** tended all of it: families who kept the lights, trained their children to
  it, and gave themselves to it at the end. Their word for themselves was **lortir**, the keeping
  people.

The dwarves of **Karn Tholl**, under the Greyhills, made the lanterns, and the crowns and blades the
kings were buried with: the "older work" in every barrow is theirs. The elves taught the rite, and
came to regret it. They held that a soul kept is a soul kept from going on, and that a kindled king
is not a counsellor but a prisoner who has forgotten he wanted to leave. Veymar did not listen, and
about a century before the end the elves stopped coming to its courts.

**Orlath**, "the gold-holder", was the richest of the barrow kings, three hundred years before the
end. His barrow, **Orlhal**, stood on a rise at the edge of the low country, and more was buried in
it than anyone has dug up since.

## The Drowning

**Merrath**, "the all-holder", was the last king of Veymar. He was old and he was afraid, not of
dying but of the barrow: he had gone down to ask his fathers things and seen what centuries in a
lantern do to a king, and he would not be kept like that. He wanted to be kept awake, and whole, and
king. So he ordered **the Great Kindling**: every kindled flame in Veymar drawn into one, in a
lantern at the top of the great light at **Marhal** (MAR-hal), "sea-hill", the capital on the one
high place on the coast, with his own living soul bound into the sum of them. Every warden and every
king in one light, the king in all of it, for ever. He said nobody would ever have to die again, and
he may have believed it.

The dwarves would not make the lantern. His lampwright, **Lorn**, made it anyway, and lit it on
midwinter's night.

Every light in Veymar went out at once, and was drawn into the one. The sea-lights went dark, and by
morning the sea had the low country. Marhal went under with its light still burning. Most of the
kingdom drowned that night, and in every barrow, a king whose lantern had gone out woke in the dark
under the water.

The Great Kindling took every light in Veymar at once, and even that was not enough to make Merrath
what he meant to be. He sleeps in it, under the sea.

## The long dark

- **The survivors** were mostly lamp-warden families, who were on the sea-wall when it failed and
  knew the kindling better than anyone. They made homes on the islands of high ground left in the
  new fen, and went down to the woken kings with new lights. A lantern needs a soul, so they gave it
  theirs: **every fenfolk who dies is kindled into a barrow's lantern**, to keep a king asleep who
  would otherwise walk. Their dead do not rest; they keep watch. They are the fenfolk, and they have
  done it for six hundred years.
- **They found the lampwright** alive, and knew what Lorn had done. They did not kill Lorn. They
  kindled Lorn, unwilling, into a small lantern and sealed it in a cell cut into the Greyhills, as
  far from the sea as they could carry it, so it could never reach the great light and never rest.
  It is the only soul in the Veymarch kindled against its will.
- **The elves** withdrew into the Stillwood and shut it.
- **The dwarves** lost their deepest halls, which ran out under the low country, to the sea the same
  night. They sealed Karn Tholl's doors and have traded with nobody since. Some of them say it was
  grief, and some say shame, since they made the lanterns; all of them say Veymar owes them for the
  halls, and a dwarf does not forget a debt.
- **The goblins** came down into the empty Greyhills after the dwarves shut their doors, and dug
  where the dwarves had stopped.
- **Aldmark** grew up in the east, over the hills from all of it. To Aldmark the west was the Drowned
  West: bad land, and ghost stories.

## The first charter

Seventy years ago the Crown of Aldmark tried the west for the first time. A village grew up round a
mill on the west road, and the settlers dammed a stream to make the millpond. The stream's head was a
shrine the elves had kept since the Drowning, where they had said goodbye to Veymar. One elf who had
never left, the one the settlers came to call the Willow Warden, asked them to break the dam. They
did not. That winter did not end: the pond froze in summer, the wheel turned backwards on still
nights, and every child in the village dreamed the same dream. After eleven years they left. Aldmark
calls it the Grey Winter, and blames goblins and a hard frost.

## The second charter

Thirty-one years ago the Crown tried again, and gave the west to a company. **The Veymarch Company**
built **Lampton** at the crossroads by the pond, round a carved stone with an iron cage on top that
the first carters took for a lamp-post: a Veymari waymarker, its light long out. Since then the
Company has opened the east road to carts, cut salt pans at the fen's northern edge, put an outpost
at **Greyford** on the west road and opened a quarry in the Greyhills. It has done all of it well,
and every piece of it has put out a light it did not know was there:

- **The salt pans** were cut across the fenfolk's channels to the southern barrows. The raids began
  within the year, and the Company has called the fenfolk raiders since.
- **The drains** that feed the pans lowered the water over Orlhal, which is how the Sunken Barrow's
  door came to be above the water for the first time in six hundred years, and how the fenfolk who
  kept Orlath's lantern lost the channel they reached it by. It guttered this summer, and Orlath
  woke.
- **The New Cut**'s third blast this spring broke into a sealed stone cell. The crew found a cracked
  lantern with nothing in it and sold it to the fettler at Greyford for beer money. That night, what
  had been in it came down the hill.

## Now

The lanterns are going out: a few because they are old, most because the Company's work cuts the
fenfolk off from them or breaks them outright, and some because people like Hollis dig up graves
for what is in them.

When a lantern goes out, the soul in it does not go on. **It is drawn to Marhal**, where Merrath's
great light is still burning under the sea and still pulling every loose soul in the Veymarch
towards it, as it was lit to. What is left in the barrow is the body, and a body kept for centuries
does not lie still when its keeping stops: it wakes empty, cold, and hungry for warmth. That is a
**wight**. A king was buried with so much of himself that a little of him stays behind in his crown
and his blade, enough to speak, to give orders and to remember a face, and that is a **barrow king**.
Orlath is the first to wake since the Drowning. He will not be the last.

Every soul that goes to Marhal feeds the light Merrath sleeps in. Six hundred years ago every light
in Veymar at once was not enough to wake him. Now they are coming to him one at a time, and each
brings him nearer to waking as what he meant to be: a king who holds everything, the Veymarch and
everyone in it, living or not, in one light.

## The climb to 20

What the story is, a band at a time. `places.md` has where. Part G sizes levels 9-20 and may change
how the story is reached, but not what it is about.

- **1-3, the starter band.** A frontier town and the country round it: rats, crabs, the Company's
  quarry, the Red Rags on the east road. The player is a nobody who came west on a Company cart that
  the Red Rags took on the way, and walked into Lampton with a rusty sword. Wick is a curiosity with
  no past.
- **4-8, the upper band.** The road west and the fen: goblins, Greyford, the fenfolk, a door in the
  Deep Cut that the goblins will not dig past, and Orlath. The grey starts to show: the raiders have
  a reason, the fettler is a fence, and the Company is the one putting the lights out. At the bottom
  of the barrow, Wick hears its name.
- **9-20, Part G.** The fenfolk's hidden holm, and what they did to Lorn; the Stillwood and Karn
  Tholl opening their doors because they know what is coming; more barrows waking; the Candles off
  the strand, and the drowned sea-wall past them out towards Marhal; and at the
  top, Merrath the Last in the great light, and the answer to what Wick did.

**The ending, as intended.** The great light can only be put out from inside, by a soul in it
willing to let go, and the one who lit it can go into it. Wick goes in and lets go, and every soul
of Veymar goes on with it: the kings, the wardens, the fenfolk's dead, and Lorn. The player walks
back up out of the sea on their own. `spirit.md` says why the bible wants it this way; whether the
player can argue with it is Part G's to decide.
