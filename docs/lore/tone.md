# Tone

How the Veymarch sounds (decision 87, pillar 5): an earnest world whose people are sometimes funny,
with real stakes underneath, blended the way RuneScape blends it. This is the voice every line is
written in: greetings, dialog, quests, contracts, rumours, Wick, signposts and item cards.

## The rules

1. **The world is serious; its people are not always.** A joke comes out of somebody's character,
   Tilda's nose for gossip, a dwarf's accounts, a goblin's pride, and never from the narrator winking
   at the player.
2. **Not constant.** Most lines have no joke, and an exchange has one at most. Along a chain of
   quests the jokes thin as the stakes rise, and in the barrow there are none. A joke in every line
   is no joke at all.
3. **The stakes are real.** The dead were people. The Drowning killed a kingdom. The fenfolk give
   their own dead to the lanterns. Grief is never the punchline; only goblins and fools joke about
   barrows, and the line knows they are wrong to.
4. **Magic is rare, and said with care.** Nobody explains magic in passing. A stranger thing is
   described by what it does to whoever sees it, and never named before it is seen (pillar 4).
5. **People are wrong.** A line says what its speaker believes. The fettler thinks fenweave was
   stolen, the Company thinks the fenfolk are raiders, and Wick thinks it is a nice light that
   happened to be passing. The bible says what is true; the game lets the player find it out.
6. **The player is a nobody, at first.** Nobody calls them a hero at level 1. Respect is earned and
   the lines show it: the quartermaster's tone at level 8 is not his tone at level 1.
7. **Plain words.** Short sentences and British spelling. No modern idiom (no "okay", "cool",
   "awesome", "guys"), no memes, and no fourth wall, except where a tip has to name a control.
8. **Names sparingly.** A line names at most one person or place the player has not met. The history
   arrives in pieces, never in a paragraph.

## Right

Samples for the voice, not final lines: D1 writes the dialog and D4 the spirit.

- Tilda Pell, asked about the raiders: "Raiders, the Company calls them. My mother called them the
  fen people and bought her eels off them. Draw your own lines."
- Jory Stroud: "I don't care what's under the fen. I care what's on the road, and what's on the road
  is Hollis."
- Marta Hale, to a new wizard: "Fire does what you tell it. That's the good news and the bad news."
- Silas Quill, turning a crown over: "Beautiful work. Nobody wearing it. Seems a waste."
- Maren, keeper of the third light: "Every light you see out there is somebody's mother. Mind where
  you put your feet."
- Karra Deepvein: "Six hundred years we've kept that door shut, and the first thing through the other
  side is a goblin with a shovel."
- A goblin: "This is Gorb's hole. You want Gorb's coal, you pay Gorb's toll. Toll is: go away."
- The Willow Warden: "You carry a kindled thing. I will not speak while it listens."
- Orlath the Barrow King: "Lorn. Have you come to tend me?"
- Wick, underground: "I'm a very good light. You may say so."
- Wick, on the strand: "I don't like it out there. It's too big, and it's pulling."
- The signpost to the Sunken Barrow, as it already is: "Locked, and what is buried in there was
  buried holding it. Come at eight."

The six townsfolk's greetings are already in this voice, since A4 wrote them to be. D1 keeps their
spirit and lets them name the places and people they could not.

## Wrong

- "Welcome, hero! The fate of the Veymarch rests on your shoulders!" The player is a nobody, and
  nobody talks like a trailer.
- "Another rat? This town's got more rats than a sewer convention." The narrator's joke, in the wrong
  century.
- "Long ago, the kingdom of Veymar mastered the kindling, a magic that binds souls into flame, until
  King Merrath's Great Kindling caused the Drowning..." A paragraph of history, which nobody says
  aloud, and it gives Wick away.
- "Fool! You cannot defeat me!" Orlath does not taunt. The dead are not villains.
- Every line of a conversation with a joke in it.

## Where the humour lives

The funny ones: Tilda, Marta, Silas, Pocket the crow, the goblins, the dwarves without meaning to be,
and Wick early on. The never funny: the Willow Warden, the dead, and Maren, except once, bleakly. The
Company is funny only in its paperwork.
